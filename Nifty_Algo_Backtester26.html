Algo Backtester

Test a trading idea on your own candles before it costs you a rupee.

Drop in an OHLC file for Nifty, Bank Nifty or any stock. Algo Backtester replays your rules candle by candle, charges brokerage and slippage, and shows you what would have happened.

Runs entirely in your browser. Your file is never uploaded.How it works

What this platform is

A backtesting workbench for rule-based intraday and swing strategies. You bring the price history; it brings the indicators, the rule engine, realistic costs and a trade-by-trade log, so you can judge an idea on evidence instead of a hunch.

Any instrument, any file

CSV with date/time, open, high, low and close is enough. Volume is optional. Delimiters, column order and date formats are detected, and messy rows are sorted, de-duplicated or skipped with a note.

Rules you can read

Combine entry and exit conditions for long and short sides, use crossovers and the previous candle, then add stop loss, target, trailing stop and end-of-day square-off.

Costs included

Slippage, brokerage per order and other charges are deducted on every trade, so the profit you see is net, with drawdown and profit factor beside it.

Two ways to build a strategy

Find best strategies for me

Choose a test period, capital, an acceptable drawdown band and the indicators to allow. A two-stage search tries every combination, then tunes settings, stops, slippage and timeframe, and ranks what survives.

I will build it myself

Pick indicators, write conditions and set risk in the workbench. After the first run every slider updates profit, drawdown and the trade log live.

From file to result

  1. Load your CSVThe panel above reads it, shows the candle count and the date span it covers.
  2. Choose how to buildLet the search find candidates or assemble your own rules.
  3. Inspect every tradeSignal time and fill price are listed separately, with filters by year and CSV export.

Indicators included

Strategies and settings are saved in this browser, and can be exported or imported as a file. A backtest describes the past only. Results depend on your data quality and the costs you enter, and they are not a promise about future returns or investment advice.
